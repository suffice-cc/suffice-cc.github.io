Part I · Section 5.2

Baselines

Forty-eight systems in eleven families share the generator backbone, the index, the pool budget, and the prompt scaffold. What differs between them is the selection interface, which is the object under test.

A comparison is only as informative as the things it holds fixed. Every system in the suite generates with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct in bfloat16 under greedy decoding with a 16,384-token input window, retrieves from the same reciprocal-rank fusion of BM25 (k₁ = 0.9, b = 0.4) and e5-base-v2 at m = 200, and receives the same prompt scaffold. Differences in the reported numbers are therefore attributable to what each system selects and when it stops, not to backbone, index, or prompting.

Three commitments in the protocol

Three features of the setup exist because specific claims in the theory would otherwise be assumed rather than tested.

  1. Per-dataset, per-seed depth tuning. Each fixed-depth baseline receives its own k drawn from {1, 3, 5, 10, 20, 50}, selected on 200 held-out questions by a lexicographic rule over AG, Acc, SUF, and then smaller k, following the best-practice protocol of Wang et al. This is not generosity. Theorem 4.13 predicts that the attainable envelope in depth is unimodal with an interior maximizer, and a claim about the shape of a depth curve cannot be tested against baselines pinned to one arbitrary depth. Bamboogle takes the median of the other datasets, because 125 questions cannot support a tuning split of its own.
  2. Matched-answer-rate abstention. Where a baseline has no native abstention we add a calibrated log-probability variant evaluated at Suffice's answer rate. Theorem A.8 claims that certification costs nothing relative to abstaining at the same rate; comparing a selective system against non-selective ones on aggregate accuracy would decide that claim on the answer rate rather than on the selection rule.
  3. Cost matching at the generator-token budget. Every comparison is reported at the token budget Suffice consumes. Proposition 3.10 shows that coverage selection delivers a smaller context than fixed top-k whenever k exceeds κm(q)·H(Mq), the common case at κm ≤ 3. Without cost matching the method would be credited for spending less rather than for selecting better.
5.2.1

The eleven families

The families are organized by where in the pipeline a system intervenes: what is scored, what is re-ordered, what is rewritten, how depth is chosen, what is kept, and what is trusted. Families 1 through 42 are the received practice the paper argues against; 43 through 48 are the four diagnostic additions described in the next section.

Theorem 4.8 predicts that families 3 through 18 share a single failure, because each emits a ranking that the system then truncates. Families 13–15 and 37–38 are set-aware in form, so whether the barrier binds them is an empirical question rather than a corollary, which is what Prediction P2 tests.

5.2.2

Four additions that test the contribution

The first forty-two systems establish that the suite is competitive. The last six exist to make specific claims falsifiable, and each is paired with the result it could refute.

#43 · Sufficient-context selective generation

The direct antecedent. Joren et al. define sufficient context, label it with an autorater, and gate generation on the label. Including it isolates what this paper adds — sufficiency as the retrieval objective and as a certified quantity — from what it inherits, which is the diagnostic lens. We run it as a logistic combination of the autorater's P(Yes) and mean log-probability, thresholded at Suffice's answer rate.

#44–45 · C-RAG and RobustRAG

The only other systems in the literature that carry guarantees, and the comparators for Theorems A.2 and A.10 respectively. They certify different objects: C-RAG bounds generation risk, RobustRAG certifies per-passage robustness, and Suffice certifies the sufficiency of the input. An equal result against these two is the expected result and not a failure, because the claim is composability and cost of the certified object, not a larger number.

#46 · Oracle-κm

A baseline handed the gold dispersion and allowed to retrieve exactly κm(q) passages by relevance. It separates the two things Suffice does at once. If Oracle-κm matched Suffice, the contribution would reduce to knowing how deep to go; the gap between them is the value of knowing which passages, not merely how many.

#47–48 · MMR and DPP in entailment space

Proposition 4.10 separates diversity from coverage by construction: MMR measures dissimilarity between documents, while coverage is measured between documents and premises. A construction is not evidence, so we also run both diversity methods over premise-entailment vectors e(·, d) rather than embeddings. These are the strongest honest competitors in the suite — set-aware, operating in the right space, and differing from coverage-greedy mainly in lacking the stopping rule and the certificate.

What this suite cannot refute

Every system is run on one 3B backbone, so absolute levels are properties of that scale and only the ordering transfers. Systems marked [reconstruction] are re-implemented from their papers rather than run from author code; a reconstruction that underperforms its published result is a fact about the reconstruction before it is a fact about the method. Finally, the suite tests selection and stopping. It cannot adjudicate claims about training-time methods (#31–34) on equal footing, because those were trained against different corpora.

Which baselines were actually run

Twenty of the forty-eight systems were run for this version, and the tables on this page report those twenty plus coverage-greedy at fixed k. All of them share the generator, index, pool budget, and prompt scaffold; #2 fits all 100 passages inside the 16k input window.

Seven deviations from the protocol as written
  • Corpora. Dataset paragraphs for 2Wiki and MuSiQue plus 5.24 M Wikipedia abstracts and StrategyQA evidence — not Wikipedia 2024-12, ClueWeb22-B or FineWeb, and no live search API. These corpora contain the gold supporting paragraphs verbatim, which raises the retrieval ceiling relative to an open-Web pool.
  • Index. Exact inner-product dense search on GPU, not HNSW or FAISS; BM25 by bm25s, a Lucene variant. Assumption 6's top-m ANN oracle is therefore exact here, so no approximation error enters πm.
  • Main operating point. On Wikipedia one article is one source, so τ ≥ 3 is almost never attainable: the answer rate is 0.4% at τ = 3 and 0% at τ ≥ 5. The Byzantine certificate and the deployed operating point are separated by the corpus, not by a tuning choice.
  • Entailment model. e(p, d) is MiniCheck on the premise claim filled with the passage's SQuAD-2 answer span; pairs with no answer at P(answerable) < 0.01 score 0 without a MiniCheck call. Contradiction ē(p, d) is nli-deberta-v3-small against the claim filled with the best-supported answer in the pool.
  • Sufficiency labels. Supporting facts for calibration and SUF; Bamboogle has none and falls back to the autorater, which is why its rows carry no source-(i) column in Table 16.
  • Drift. No temporal split exists in these corpora, so Theorem A.5 is tested with a covariate shift towards tail entities rather than with time.
  • Sensitivity. α, τ and verifier noise η were varied; pool size m and the greedy tolerance ε were not.
AG is not measuring what the paper wants it to measure

The attribution judge is nli-deberta-v3-small evaluated on the hypothesis "{question} {answer}". It accepts an answer only rarely, so AG is near zero for every system in the suite, including the baselines, and the AG column cannot separate them. An earlier run with DeBERTa-v3-large gave SUF ≈ 0.6 × Acc, which is the behaviour a working judge produces.

Every AG-derived quantity inherits this: SNA, AG per 1k tokens, the AG@matched column of Table 4, the AG rows of the paired bootstrap in Table 25, and the AG columns of the ablation. Acc, SUF, CSR, Ans%, Acc|answered and CAS are computed independently of the judge and are unaffected. Until the judge is replaced, conclusions on this page that rest on AG — including the "significantly lower AG" rows of Table 25 — should be read as statements about the judge's threshold rather than about grounding.

Part II · Section 6

Results

Suffice is not the highest-accuracy system in the suite, and it is not meant to be. It is the only system whose answer rate is a controlled variable rather than a fixed constant, and the results below are organized around what that control buys and what it costs.

Reading the main table requires one convention stated up front. Γ = Acc − AG is the grounding gap: answers that are correct without being attributable to the delivered evidence. A system with high Acc and high Γ is answering from parametric memory while displaying a context it did not use. Every baseline in the suite answers 100% of questions, so for them CSR — the probability of answering on insufficient context — is not a free parameter but a consequence, and it sits above 70%.

Three readings follow from the table. First, the corroboration level behaves as Proposition A.11 says it must: the certified answer rate collapses monotonically across τ = 1, 3, 5, 7 — 13.3%, 1.8%, 0.6%, 0.3% — while SUF stays flat at 28–29% and CAS rises from 59.4 to 69.0. Tightening τ does not improve selection; it narrows the set of questions on which the system is willing to speak, and what survives is better grounded. Second, the entailment-space diversity baselines (#47–48) are the strongest competitors on AG and SUF, which is the honest shape of the result: moving diversity into the right space recovers most of the selection benefit, and what remains is the stopping rule and the certificate. Third, every non-selective system reports an answer rate of exactly 100% at every depth, which is the CSR ≥ 70.9% regime that the always-answer ablation makes explicit.

Every cell satisfies the guarantee, and the empirical risk tracks α closely at τ = 1 (1.80, 4.73, 8.92 against targets of 5, 10, 20) rather than sitting far below it — the certificate is tight, not conservative. At τ ≥ 3 the system abstains on essentially everything on these four datasets, so τ = 1 across α is the informative operating line and the τ ≥ 3 rows are supporting evidence for the robustness claim rather than a deployment recommendation.

6.2

The certificate is load-bearing

An architecture with eight stages invites the suspicion that most of them are decoration. The ablation is run at the deployed operating point (α = 0.10, τ = 1) and reports CSR, which is the quantity the guarantee is about, alongside SUF and the answer rate that pays for it.

The components separate into three groups by what they move. Removing the coverage objective is the largest single loss, cutting SUF from 28.2 to 14.6 under top-k and to 7.4 under MMR — the independence barrier of Theorem 4.8, measured as an ablation rather than as a construction. Removing premise decomposition costs nearly as much (16.3), which is expected, since without premises there is nothing for coverage to cover. Removing the conformal halt or constrained decoding raises CSR above the target while raising the answer rate, which is the failure mode the certificate exists to prevent: a system that answers more and is wrong more while reporting the same SUF. Removing abstention is the limit of that behavior at CSR 70.9%.

Two components move almost nothing at this operating point. Conflict detection and minimal-core extraction leave CSR and SUF within noise. That is the correct result for both: conflict detection fires only on contested questions, of which there are 11 in the 200-item audit sample, and minimal-core extraction changes which passages are shown, not whether the system answers. Their value is tested by P5 and P7 respectively, not here.

6.3 · Prediction P1

The depth envelope

P1
AG does not exceed the envelope U(k), and for every family its empirical maximizer over fixed k is interior and increases with dispersion stratum.

Theorem 4.13 bounds AG by U(k) = σ(k)(1 − β)u(k) and proves that U — the envelope, not AG itself — is strictly unimodal. Remark 1 is load-bearing here: unimodality of an upper bound does not imply unimodality of the bounded quantity, so the prediction is stated as a containment claim plus an interiority claim, and the comparative statics in κm are labelled a modelling step rather than a derivation.

The containment half holds: AG stays under the envelope at every tested depth on every family but one, where it holds at 94% of depths. The interiority half holds only where it is testable. k★ is interior for the hybrid family on 2Wiki (k = 20) and StrategyQA (k = 3) and boundary elsewhere. On MuSiQue AG is near zero across the sweep, so the argmax is a tie broken by the lexicographic rule and carries no information about curvature; we report k★ = 1 and decline to interpret it. A boundary maximizer at k = 50 on 2Wiki covorder is a genuine partial failure of P1 and, per Section 7, the cost is Assumption 2: if distraction does not bite, Theorem 4.13 collapses to "retrieve more," leaving certification, the barrier, and the robustness result standing.

The synthetic check separates the two issues. On simulated envelopes the maximizer is interior at every dispersion level and the slope changes sign exactly once, as the theorem requires, and it moves right as κ grows, which is the comparative static the empirical table cannot establish.

6.4 · Prediction P2

The redundancy barrier

P2
On high-redundancy slices, set-oblivious rerankers lose more SUF relative to Suffice than on low-redundancy slices, with the gap growing in r(q); embedding-space MMR tracks the rerankers, and entailment-space MMR and DPP close most but not all of the gap.

This is the direct test of Theorem 4.8. Measured redundancy r(q) is the maximum number of pool passages entailing any single premise, so a slice with r > 15 is one where a scorer that cannot see sets has fifteen chances to fill its budget with the same fact.

The prediction holds in its qualitative shape and fails in one quantitative detail. Coverage-greedy leads at every redundancy level and embedding-space MMR collapses to 6.1% at high redundancy, which is Proposition 4.10 behaving as constructed: diversity in embedding space selects passages that are far apart and entail nothing. Entailment-space MMR and DPP close most of the gap at low and middling redundancy and in fact overtake coverage-greedy in the 3–5 band, then fall behind again above r > 15. The part of P2 that does not hold cleanly is monotonicity of the gap: hybrid RRF is roughly flat across bands (31.8 → 24.1) rather than degrading steeply, so the advantage over it narrows at the top band instead of widening. On the reading of Section 7, that means real corpora at this pool size are less adversarially redundant than the r-duplicate construction assumes — the theorem stands and is non-binding at m = 200.

6.5 · Predictions P3 and P8

Marginal and conditional risk

P3
CSR ≤ α in-distribution for α ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.20}; under temporal shift without weighted correction, CSR exceeds α by at most the measured δ. We commit in advance to reporting the violation.
P8
Marginal certification violates α in at least one stratum, while Mondrian calibration restores per-stratum validity at a measurable answer-rate cost.

P3 is the headline prediction, because failing it in-distribution voids the guarantee rather than weakening it. The test is run over 500 random calibration/test splits per seed so that the reported CSR is a distribution rather than a single draw, and the share of splits satisfying the bound is reported alongside the mean.

In distribution, mean realised CSR sits at or just under α on every dataset and level. The share-of-splits column is the honest qualifier: the guarantee is marginal over the calibration draw, so 13% to 42% of individual splits exceed α at α = 0.10, which is what a marginal guarantee means and not a violation of it. The q95 column makes the tail legible — at α = 0.10 on 2Wiki the 95th percentile split realises 0.12.

Under shift towards tail entities, uncorrected CRC stays inside α + δ everywhere, as Theorem A.5 predicts, and does so with a large margin: at δ = 0.35 the bound is 0.45 and realised risk is 0.072. The drift penalty is therefore loose in practice, which is the expected behaviour of a total-variation bound and not evidence of a tighter theorem. The weighted correction helps on Bamboogle, cutting mean CSR from 0.09 to 0.046 and the worst stratum from 0.32 to 0.15, and slightly hurts on 2Wiki (0.072 → 0.087) where the estimated likelihood ratio is noisier than the shift it corrects.

For P8 the worst-stratum column is the one to read. Marginal calibration leaves at least one stratum far above α on every dataset — 0.45 on 2Wiki against a target of 0.10 — which is the prediction, and the point of making it in advance. Mondrian strata close most of that gap.

The answer-rate column prices conditional validity exactly as Theorem A.7 says it must: at α = 0.05 the marginal rule answers 99.4% of questions with a worst stratum at 3.3× the target, while the Mondrian rule answers 74.4% with the worst stratum at 1.09×. Twenty-five points of coverage is what per-stratum validity costs at the strictest level, and the whole of that cost falls on the strata where the marginal rule was borrowing risk from the majority. Exact conditional coverage is impossible distribution-free, so the stratification — dispersion band × language × popularity × contestedness, merged below 50 points — is a declared commitment about which subpopulations matter rather than a technical convenience.

6.6 · Prediction P4

Where the sufficiency labels come from

P4
Γ > 0 for every baseline, largest for closed-book and long-context stuffing, and our Γ estimates agree with Joren et al. to within annotation noise.

CSR is measured against a label that is itself partly model-derived, so the label's reliability is not a methodological footnote but a bound on how much any risk number can mean. We compare three automatic sources: (i) dataset supporting facts, (ii) the Joren autorater run on Qwen2.5-3B, and (iii) a three-model NLI majority ensemble.

The negative result we are reporting

Raw agreement between the autorater and the NLI ensemble is high (0.87 to 0.98), and Cohen's κ between them is approximately zero. Both facts are true at once because both sources label almost everything insufficient: the autorater's positive rate is 0.1% to 2.3%, so two raters who nearly always say "no" agree nearly always and carry nearly no information. Krippendorff's α among the three NLI voters is negative on three of four datasets, which means the individual voters disagree more than chance and the ensemble's majority rule — not its members — is doing the work.

The consequence for the rest of the paper is specific rather than fatal. CSR is measured against an imperfect oracle with a known bias towards declaring insufficiency, so reported CSR is conservative where the oracle is strict, and absolute SUF levels should not be compared across papers that use a different labeller. Relative comparisons within the suite, which is what every table here reports, hold because all systems are scored by the same labeller. The 200-item human audit exists for exactly this reason and is reported in the appendix rather than used to adjust any number.

6.7 · Prediction P5

Byzantine-certified robustness

P5
Attack success is exactly zero for all b + f ≤ b★ from the certified-robustness audit and rises sharply above it; RobustRAG shows a zero-success regime at a per-passage rather than per-source threshold, so the two curves cross.

Theorem A.10 certifies answer invariance exactly when b + f < τ/2, where b counts injected passages and f counts compromised independent sources. The factor of two is the Byzantine one: a compromised source removes a genuine attestation and supplies an adversarial one in the same move.

The crossing predicted by P5 is visible at b = 1 versus b = 2. RobustRAG's per-passage isolation holds a single injected passage to 13.2% success and then fails to 51.9% at two, because isolation defeats a handful of crafted documents and not a coordinated set. Source-granular corroboration degrades far more slowly in b and is nearly flat in f at τ ≥ 3, because compromising distinct outlets is the expensive axis for the adversary and the one the certificate is written against. The two defences are complementary rather than competing, and neither answers an adversary who genuinely operates τ/2 independent outlets.

The sharper test is whether the certificate holds where it claims to, and here the audit does not return what P5 predicted. Pooling all seeds, 12 question-condition pairs are classified as lying inside the certified radius, all of them at τ = 3, and attack success on those 12 is 25%. Three certified answers were flipped by an adversary the certificate declared could not flip them. Everything outside the radius behaves as expected, falling from 6.82% at τ = 1 to 0.04% at τ = 7.

Two readings of the 12 inside-radius cases

Either the certificate is unsound, or the audit's radius is being computed wrongly. Two things point at the second. First, no condition at τ = 5 or τ = 7 registers as inside the radius at all, although the bounds grid below says that b ≤ 2 and f ≤ 1 are certified at τ = 5, so the audit should be finding thousands of inside-radius pairs there and finds none. Second, the per-variant b★ values logged by the ablation run are fractional (0.10, 0.15, 0.20), while b★ is a count of passages and must be a non-negative integer.

On the reading Section 7 commits to in advance, a failure at b + f ≤ b★ means Assumption 8 is violated, the adversary controls outlets our clustering treats as independent, or the clustering is too coarse: the theorem stands and the implementation does not. The fractional radii make a third explanation more likely than either, which is that Algorithm 3 is returning a scaled quantity rather than ⌈τmin/2⌉ − 1. This needs resolving before submission, because P5 is stated as an exact-zero claim and the audit as logged refutes it.

The grid is included because it is hand-checkable, and because the τ = 1 block is uniformly false. At the deployed operating point the corroboration rule certifies nothing against a Byzantine adversary; robustness begins at τ = 3, where Table 4 shows the answer rate has already fallen to 1.8%. That tension — the operating point with usable coverage and the operating point with a robustness certificate are not the same point — is the paper's main unresolved cost, and Proposition A.11 says it is exactly the probability that some premise has fewer than τ independent attestations.

6.8 · Prediction P6

Verifier noise and the attainability frontier

P6
Suffice's (risk, answer-rate) points lie on its verifier's ROC frontier to within sampling error, and AG degrades with injected entailment noise at the 2ηκm rate of Theorem 4.5, independent of k. If degradation scales with k, the theorem is wrong.

This is the cleanest falsification test in the set, because it names the quantity whose behaviour would refute the theorem. We inject calibrated noise into the entailment model at η ∈ {0.10, 0.20} and regress coverage loss on log k. A positive slope refutes Theorem 4.5.

Every fitted slope lies between −0.007 and 0. Coverage loss does not grow with depth at either noise level on any dataset, which is the prediction and is the non-obvious half of Theorem 4.5: the per-step error of 2η is damped by the same geometric contraction that drives the coverage bound, so the penalty sums to 2ηκm and stops there. The operational consequence is Corollary 4.6 — deepening the budget is free in this bound, and improving the entailment model is what buys harder questions.

The verifier comparison also shows where the headroom is. SQuAD-2 answerability reaches the highest AUC (0.98) and the lowest calibration error on 2Wiki but delivers SUF of 0.49 against MiniCheck's 0.62, so AUC alone does not order the verifiers by what matters. Theorem A.9 explains why: the achievable (risk, answer-rate) region is the convex region under the ROC curve, and AUC is one scalar functional of that curve. Two verifiers with equal AUC can offer different answer rates at a given α.

6.9 · Prediction P7

Are certificates causal?

P7
CAS is high for Suffice and low for baselines whose answers are merely post-hoc attributed; certified answers change when their minimal core is removed.

A certificate that names evidence the generator did not use is decorative. CAS measures the fraction of certified answers that change when the minimal support core S† is withheld, which makes the question empirical rather than definitional. This is, to our knowledge, untested by any existing attribution metric.

Roughly 59 to 60% of certified answers change when the core is removed, and that figure rises to 69.0% at τ = 7. The complement is the uncomfortable part: on about 40% of certified answers the generator produces the same output from parametric memory, and for those the certificate is valid and causally inert. Section 7 named this in advance as the paper's most interesting possible negative result, and a partial version of it has occurred.

The ΔAcc column needs its caveat read. Accuracy without the core (32.0%) exceeds accuracy with it (7.7%) by 24 points, which looks like evidence that the evidence hurts. It is not: the two columns have different denominators, because Acc is computed over all questions including the 87% on which Suffice abstains, while Acc w/o core is computed on the answered subset. The correct comparison is against Acc|answered, which is 25.0 ± 17.7 at τ = 3 and 66.7 ± 8.3 at τ = 5. We report the mixed-denominator column because it appears in the ablation logs and would otherwise invite exactly this misreading from a reader who found it there.

6.10

The budget the guarantee actually spends

Theorem A.6 refuses to assume away the two things that stand between a certificate over recovered premises and a claim about the truth: premise recall and first-stage pool recall. It prices them as α + Mqεd + πm and requires both to be measured. They are, and the result is unflattering by design.

The binding limitation

The end-to-end bound is vacuous on all three datasets: 0.77 on 2Wiki and above 1.0 on MuSiQue and StrategyQA. Keeping the two correction terms under α = 0.10 at Mq = 4 would require premise recall above roughly 97.5% and pool miss below 2.5%, and the decomposer reaches 78.6% at best and 51.2% at worst. What is certified in every other table on this page is therefore the sufficiency of the retrieved pool with respect to the recovered premise set, which is a real and checkable property, and not sufficiency with respect to the true premise set over the corpus. Premise extraction and pool recall are priced here, not solved.

The cost picture has two sides and the table reports both. Suffice sends 110 prompt tokens per question against dense top-k's 2,072 — the prediction of Proposition 3.10, since the budget κ̂mH(Mq) lands near 6 while the tuned k often lands at 50 — and reaches 0.045 AG per 1k generator tokens against 0.004. Depth efficiency is 2.65 against 8.04, meaning the delivered set is 2.65× the gold dispersion rather than 8×.

Against that, the verifier spends 336 entailment calls per question, which Corollary 4.12 says is unimprovable up to constants given the Ω(mMq) lower bound. Those calls are small-model batched passes and cacheable across queries sharing passages, but a reader who counts total FLOPs rather than generator tokens will reach a different verdict than this table gives, and we state the accounting rather than choose it. Adaptive allocation is what makes the figure 336 rather than the 422 to 778 calls the non-adaptive schedule requires.

6.11

Paired bootstrap and the honest reading

Significance is assessed by a paired cluster bootstrap with 10,000 resamples over dataset, seed, and question, with Holm–Bonferroni correction across the 48 comparisons per metric. Unanswered questions count as zero, which is the conservative convention for a system that abstains on 87% of its input.

What the bootstrap says, stated plainly

Suffice has significantly lower AG than every set-oblivious baseline and than both entailment-space diversity variants, and significantly lower Acc than all of them, because it abstains on most questions and abstentions score zero. It is statistically indistinguishable from the three systems that also withhold answers or carry guarantees — Oracle-κ, RobustRAG-style, and the sufficient-context selector. It beats closed-book on AG by 0.24 points, which is the only positive row.

The contribution is therefore the certificate and the depth law, not a uniform SUF or AG win, and the paper should not be read as claiming one. A reader who wants the single number that distinguishes this system from the suite should take CSR: 4.73% against a 10% target, where every non-selective baseline sits at 70.9% and has no mechanism for moving.

Experimental setup as run

Mondrian strata, fixed in advance

A stratum is the product of four pre-retrieval variables, so that membership is determined before any passage is scored and the exchangeability argument inside each stratum survives. Dispersion band is κ̂m bucketed at < 1.5, < 2.5, ≥ 2.5. Language is English when the ASCII-letter share is at least 0.9. Entity popularity is the rarest-entity BM25 document frequency, split head/tail at the auxiliary-split median. Contestedness is the decomposer's own pre-retrieval prediction. A stratum with fewer than 50 calibration points is merged with its neighbour in the order band → language → popularity → contestedness, which is stricter than the ⌈1/α⌉ − 1 minimum Theorem A.7 requires.

Part IV

Reproducibility

Three stacks, not one

The results on this page come from three separate runs with different hardware and, in two of the three, a different generator and entailment model. The paper currently describes one environment. Before submission these should be named as distinct suites, because a reader who takes the Qwen2.5-3B setup description and then reads the HotpotQA, PopQA, NQ and TriviaQA numbers will be reading results produced by flan-t5-base and MiniLM cosine similarity, not by the system the setup section describes.

The cross-domain suite runs N = 300 per domain with Ncal = Ntest = 150, a retrieval pool of |Eq| = 20 passages per query, and a conformal grid of λ ∈ [0, 1] over 500 uniform points at α ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.20}. It evaluates ten baselines rather than forty-eight: closed-book, BM25 Okapi, dense bi-encoder, hybrid RRF (c = 60), embedding MMR (λ = 0.5), HyDE, a Self-RAG-style adaptive router, RobustRAG, entailment-space MMR, and Suffice. Its zero-leakage audit reports 0 of 600 label leakages under blind premise decomposition.

Both secondary suites fix a single global seed of 42 across the Python standard library, NumPy, PyTorch CPU and PyTorch CUDA, and both use IEEE 754 float32 for dense vectors and generator activations to remove quantization variance.

Prompts, verbatim

Every prompt is sent through the Qwen2.5 chat template. Decoding is greedy with at most 32 new tokens for answers. Placeholders in braces are filled per request; passages are rendered as [i] title: text and truncated to 600 characters each.

Four of the seven promised items are not prompts

Appendix D of the paper promises verbatim prompts for seven components. Three of them are prompted: premise decomposition, the query rewrites used by HyDE and Step-Back, and certificate-constrained decoding. The other four are not LLM calls at all. Dispersion estimation is a light regressor over query features; entailment and contradiction scoring are model heads with fixed inputs; independence clustering is a deterministic procedure over publisher and near-duplicate signatures; and conflict adjudication is a graph computation over mutually contradicting sufficient subsets.
